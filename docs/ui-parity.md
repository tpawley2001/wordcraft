How WordCraft looks and feels next to Word: ribbon, dialogs, panes, keyboard, mouse and touch.
Typing behaviour has its own checklist ([`typing-parity.md`](typing-parity.md)). All assets are our
own (`AGENTS.md`: no Microsoft iconography, ever).

Document title opens Rename Document; the account badge opens Options › General for user name and initials. Saved documents retain their format and refuse conflicting names; desktop renaming requires a filesystem that supports hard links. Web renaming changes the next download name. An unsaved document's name is only the name Save suggests: its Title property is left alone.

## Find and Replace: normal caret codes

Without Use wildcards or Regular expressions, `^nnn` without a leading zero finds codes 1–127 (including controls); higher values find nothing. `^0nnn` keeps Windows-1252 decoding, while `^u` reads a Unicode scalar in decimal, with leading zeros allowed. `^?` matches characters and paragraph marks across paragraphs, skipping optional hyphens. `^p` and `^?` also find the final story mark: Find selects to the text end, replacement inserts before the retained mark, and `^&` excludes that final mark. Objects: `^f` finds footnote reference marks, `^e` endnote marks, `^2` either, `^g` drawings only, `^d` a whole field (the outermost of nested ones) only while View › Field Codes is on, and `^a` / `^5` nothing, as Microsoft 365 Word's Find does not stop at comment anchors; a field is one character to `^?`, and bookmark, comment, permission and content-control anchors are not characters at all, so a word with a bookmark inside it is still found (P0509).

Fields (P0587, from storytold/wordcraft#5560): with field codes hidden, Find, Reading Highlight and the Navigation pane read a simple field as the text it shows (a citation's `[7]`, a REF or SEQ result, a legacy text or drop-down form field's text), and a match may run from the paragraph's text into it and out again; the code is not searched. Fields whose text depends on the page (PAGE, NUMPAGES, SECTION, SECTIONPAGES; PAGEREF and STYLEREF in headers and footers), check boxes and EQ overstrikes stay one object. With field codes shown Find searches the code text, a match never crosses the field's begin or end mark, and the braces drawn around the code are not characters (`{ QUOTE` finds nothing). A match in a field selects the whole field; Replace moves past it and Replace All leaves it and reports it as skipped, rather than deleting the field. View › Field Codes searches the listed results and Reading Highlight again.

## Find and Replace: regular expressions (#4177)

With **Regular expressions** on (Search Options), Replace with expands capture groups: `$1`…`$n`, `${n}`, `${name}` and `$name` (the longest run of letters, digits and `_`), `$0` / `${0}` (the whole match), and `\1`…`\9` as in wildcard Replace; `$$` is a dollar sign, `\t` a tab, `\\` a backslash, `\$` and `\^` a `$` or `^` as typed, and `\n` a paragraph mark, which splits the paragraph as `^p` does (Replace All stays one undo step). Word's `^&`, `^p`, `^t`, `^c` and the other `^` codes work there too (with Special characters off, only the `^` codes are taken literally). A group that doesn't exist, didn't take part in the match or is out of range expands to nothing; a `$` that starts no group, an unclosed `${`, and other `\` escapes stay as written. Plain and wildcard Replace are unchanged. Word has no regular-expression mode; this follows the Rust `regex` crate's Replace syntax.

## Find and Replace: Use wildcards as in Word

`@` (one or more) takes as few characters as it can, as in Word (`a@a` in `aaab` finds `aa`); inside a `( )` group it takes as many, so `([A-Z][a-z]@)` still captures a whole word for `\1`. `{n,}` and `{n,m}` stay greedy. `{,n}` has no lower bound and is refused. `?`, `*` and `[!…]` also match a paragraph mark, so a match can run into the next paragraph (`b?c` finds `b¶c`), but never out of a table cell or story.

## Find and Replace: Match diacritics, kashida and alef hamza (#4800)

Search Options has **Match diacritics**, **Match kashida** and **Match alef hamza** (`edit.find`, `edit.findNext`, `edit.findPrevious`, `edit.advancedFind`, `edit.replace` and `edit.replaceAll` take `matchDiacritics`, `matchKashida` and `matchAlefHamza`). All three start checked, so a search matches exactly, as WordCraft always did; an option you clear sticks for the following searches, and the options open already shown when one is cleared. With Match diacritics off, `cafe` finds `café` (precomposed or with a combining accent), `ελληνικα` finds `Ελληνικά`, `cacak` finds `Čačak`, `dorde` finds `Đorđe`, `tieng viet` finds `Tiếng Việt`, and Hebrew points and cantillation and Arabic harakat are skipped. Match kashida off skips the Arabic tatweel (`كـتاب` = `كتاب`); Match alef hamza off reads `أ إ آ ٱ` as `ا`. The hamza on an alef belongs to Match alef hamza, not to diacritics. A match covers the original characters, with the marks after its last letter, so Replace All replaces whole letters and `^&` is the text as written. Use wildcards and Regular expressions always match these exactly, and the three boxes grey out with them. What counts as a diacritic: `crates/engine/src/cmd/find_fold.rs`. Not compared with Word on this machine: Word for Mac shows these options only with a right-to-left editing language enabled, and its defaults there were not observed; WordCraft shows them always.
